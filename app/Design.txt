1. The Design on the "Today" Screen
Instead of ordering the daily list strictly by chronological minutes (which makes a delayed task feel like a failure), the tasks are grouped into Time Blocks defined by the anchors, with the suggested time acting as a subtle guide.

The Card Layout
Each task card in the vertical list will feature a two-line contextual header:

Primary text (The Anchor): Bold and prominent (e.g., ☕ After Morning Coffee).

Secondary text (The Suggested Time): A lighter, smaller font just below or beside it (e.g., Target: 9:30 AM).

Dynamic Re-ordering (Smart Sorting)
The app should automatically sort the anchor groups based on the suggested times.

If "☀️ Morning Routine" has a suggested time of 7:30 AM, and "☕ After Coffee" is set for 9:30 AM, the Morning Routine group sits gracefully at the top.

The Psychological Buffer: If it's 9:45 AM and "After Coffee" isn't checked yet, the app does not turn the text bright red or flash "OVERDUE" (which triggers anxiety). Instead, it remains calm. The suggestion is there as an intention, not a strict deadline.

2. How the User Sets This Up (The UX Flow)
When creating a repeatable practice in the Journeys tab, the time/anchor picker should feel cohesive, not like two separate, annoying forms.

We can design a sleek, two-step interactive row:

Step 1: Choose the Anchor (The "When"): A horizontal scrolling carousel of beautiful icon chips (e.g., ☀️ Morning, ☕ After Coffee, 🍔 Lunch Break, 🚗 Commute, 🌙 Wind Down).

Step 2: Refine with Time (The "Around What Time"): Once an anchor is tapped, a small time-picker pill expands next to it, defaulting to a logical time for that anchor (e.g., selecting ☕ After Coffee suggests 9:00 AM). The user can tap it to tweak the specific hour.

The resulting sentence reads naturally in the UI:

"I want to practice this [ ☕ After Coffee ] around [ 9:30 AM ]."
